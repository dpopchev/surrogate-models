\subsection{Candidate Algorithms and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:algorithms}

Every surrogate in this work is trained and scored through one pipeline, so the models compared in Section~\ref{sec:results} differ only in the estimator. This section fixes that pipeline and its reference model, a plain multilayer perceptron, and reports how well it predicts the neutron-star mass.

% Settings below are the [methodology.algorithms] values of paper.toml.
\paragraph{One pipeline serves every model.}
Each model reads the prepared tables through the same design: the inputs and targets of Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}, one model per target, and the frozen split of whole curves. The inputs are standardized with statistics fitted on the training curves alone, and the target is standardized the same way and turned back before any error is measured, so no information from a held-out curve reaches the model.

\paragraph{The baseline is a plain multilayer perceptron.}
The reference network has three fully connected hidden layers of 64 units with ReLU activations and is trained on the mean squared error of the standardized target with AdamW, at a learning rate of $10^{-3}$ under a cosine schedule and in batches of 1024 rows. It is deliberately standard: it is the control against which the loss and activation choices of $H_2$ and the residual architecture of $H_3$ are measured (Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}).

\paragraph{Early stopping watches held-out curves, not held-out rows.}
A fifth of the training curves is set aside for validation, and training stops once the validation loss has not improved for 20 epochs; the weights of the best epoch are restored. Validating on randomly chosen rows instead would let the network see neighbours of every validation row during training, since rows of one curve are each other's nearest neighbours (Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}), and would stop it late.

\paragraph{Errors are relative and on the target's own scale.}
Models are compared by the mean absolute relative error, $\mathrm{MARE} = \langle |\hat{y} - y| / |y| \rangle$, on the target's original scale ($M$ in $\Msun$), with the root mean squared error alongside.

\paragraph{The baseline predicts the neutron-star mass to a MARE of $\baseNsMassMare$ on unseen curves.}
On the $\prepNsTestCurves$ test curves the network reaches a MARE of $\baseNsMassMare$ and an RMSE of $\baseNsMassRmse\,\Msun$ (\cref{fig:alg-parity}); training stopped after \baseNsMassEpochs{} epochs, a few minutes on a single CPU. Section~\ref{sec:h3} widens the comparison to Gaussian process regression, XGBoost and residual networks.

\paragraph{Across the four dataset and target pairs, the nearest curve is the bar to beat.}
The same pipeline fits all four pairs: the mass $M$ and the charge target $Y = \log_{10}(\Dch/M)$ of neutron stars and of black holes. Each pair is scored on the frozen test curves and on the $\prepNsFolds$ GroupKFold folds, where a fresh model fitted on the other folds gives one MARE per fold and the table reports their mean and spread (\cref{tab:baseline}). Two reference predictors are scored on the same curves: the mean of the training target, and the nearest curve, which returns the target of the closest training row in the standardized inputs. The mean shows that every model learns the structure of the data; the nearest curve shows how much of it a plain lookup already recovers. The charge rows score the charge itself, $\Dch$ rebuilt with the true mass from the predicted $Y$. Because the rows along a curve are dense and neighbouring curves differ little, the nearest curve predicts the mass and the charge of both datasets on the test curves more accurately than the baseline network; only on the neutron-star folds is the network's charge error the lower of the two, by about the spread of its own fold scores. A surrogate therefore earns its place by beating this lookup, or by matching it while staying smooth enough for the likelihood surface of Section~\ref{sec:mcmc}.

\paragraph{The charge is judged with the true mass, and end to end with the predicted one.}
The charge model predicts $Y$, so the charge itself, $\Dch = M\,10^{Y}$, needs a mass. Rebuilt with the true $M$, the error isolates the charge model, and it is the number $H_1$ is judged by: a MARE in $\Dch$ of $\baseNsChargeMareTrueM$ for neutron stars and $\baseBhChargeMareTrueM$ for black holes on the test curves. Rebuilt with the mass model's prediction, as the inference of Section~\ref{sec:mcmc} will use it, the MARE becomes $\baseNsChargeMarePredM$ and $\baseBhChargeMarePredM$.
