The search of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} compares candidates of very different kinds -- a lookup, an interpolation, a Gaussian process, boosted trees, networks. The comparison is fair only if everything but the candidate is fixed, so every candidate passes through one harness and is judged by one scorecard.

\paragraph{The harness fixes everything but the candidate.}
A candidate is reduced to a single operation: given the rows to fit, the validation rows and a seed, it returns a predictor. The harness owns the rest. It holds the frozen test curves and folds of Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}; it splits every training set into fit curves and validation curves, whole curves and the same ones for every candidate, so validation rows are never fit rows and a network early-stops on exactly the curves a Gaussian process may tune on; it runs every numerical library on one thread, so that times compare; it repeats each stochastic fit over seeds; and it times the fit, a call for a single input and a call for a batch the size of an ensemble of MCMC walkers.

\paragraph{The scorecard measures what the inference needs.}
A prediction is scored by its relative error on the held-out rows: the error of $M$, and for the charge the error of $\Dch$ rebuilt with the true mass, so that the charge target $Y = \log_{10}(\Dch/M)$ is judged on the quantity the inference uses. The errors are summarized by their median, 95th percentile and maximum, and read as significant figures, $-\log_{10}$ of the relative error, the unit of the data ceilings of Sections~\ref{sec:eda-ns} and~\ref{sec:eda-bh}. They are reported per zone -- the interior test curves, the edge curves held out of a refit, the rows at the physical edge -- and per decade of $\Dch$, because a mean over six orders of magnitude hides the weakest charges. Beside the error stands the ripple, the root mean square of the second difference of the residual across neighbouring grid values of the curve parameters, which a smooth surrogate keeps near zero and a lookup does not.

\paragraph{The search eliminates candidates in a fixed order.}
The representation is chosen first, at equal model, because it carries across families (Section~\ref{sec:representation}). The families are then screened at a small fixed budget each, and the best two or three per dataset and target survive by successive halving (Section~\ref{sec:families}). The survivors are tuned over their own hyperparameters, each search under a time budget of $\knobTuningBudgetMinutes$ minutes -- for networks the width, the depth, the activation, the loss (the mean squared error against the Huber loss~\citep{Huber1964} of $H_2$) and the schedule of AdamW~\citep{Loshchilov2017_AdamW} with cosine annealing~\citep{TODO_SGDR} -- and then given the precision regime: double precision, a second-order L-BFGS stage after AdamW~\citep{Michaud2023_PrecisionML}, and a second model fitted to the residuals of the first (Section~\ref{sec:precision}). At every step a change is kept only when its gain exceeds the spread of the fold and seed scores; a change within that spread is reported and dropped.

\paragraph{Every tuned candidate is scored in three zones and passes four gates.}
The gates are defined in the main text (Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}); this paragraph gives their numbers. The zones split the held-out rows: the interior test curves; the edge curves of the coupling grid, scored from a refit made without them (Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}); and the rows at the physical edge, nearest the existence edge of a black-hole curve and the stable edge of a neutron-star curve. \Cref{tab:zones} gives every tuned candidate of \cref{tab:tuning} in each zone beside the ceiling of its pair, and four gates read from it. The physics gate counts the reversed steps of the test curves, those whose predicted slope has another sign than the slope of the data, and counts a flat step, a predicted difference of zero where the data move, apart, since the prediction of boosted trees is piecewise constant along a curve. The interior gate is the one score of Section~\ref{sec:error-metric}, the interior figures against the ceiling. The edge gate sets the margin of a map (Section~\ref{sec:validity}): it holds on the black holes, \zoneBhMassMargin{} on the mass and \zoneBhChargeMargin{} on the charge, and fails on the neutron stars, \zoneNsMassMargin{} on the mass and \zoneNsChargeMargin{} on the charge, where the extrapolation did not hold. The cost gate is the time budget of $\knobTuningBudgetMinutes$ minutes a search, recorded as the verdict of each candidate (Section~\ref{sec:precision}). A candidate too slow for a search is put on fixed points or cut, never dropped unreported. On fixed points are the pointwise interpolant, whose every query solves its own system on its neighbours, on the neutron-star mass (verdict \tunNsMassRbfPointwiseVerdict{}, $\tunNsMassRbfPointwiseTrials$ neighbour counts), on the neutron-star charge (\tunNsChargeRbfPointwiseVerdict{}, $\tunNsChargeRbfPointwiseTrials$) and on the black-hole charge (\tunBhChargeRbfPointwiseVerdict{}, $\tunBhChargeRbfPointwiseTrials$), and the residual network on the neutron-star charge (\tunNsChargeResnetPointwiseVerdict{}, $\tunNsChargeResnetPointwiseTrials$ point), whose search of an earlier batch held the tuning for hours. Every other candidate ran its search inside the budget, verdict searched, and none is cut: too slow.
