The search of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} compares candidates of very different kinds -- a lookup, an interpolation, a Gaussian process, boosted trees, networks. Every candidate passes through one harness and is judged by one scorecard.

\paragraph{The harness fixes everything but the candidate.}
A candidate is reduced to a single operation: given the rows to fit, the validation rows and a seed, it returns a predictor. The harness owns the rest. It holds the frozen test curves and folds of Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}; it splits every training set into fit curves and validation curves, whole curves and the same ones for every candidate, so validation rows are never fit rows and a network early-stops on exactly the curves a Gaussian process may tune on; it runs every numerical library on one thread, so that times compare; it repeats each stochastic fit over seeds; and it times the fit, a call for a single input and a call for a batch the size of an ensemble of MCMC walkers.

\paragraph{The scorecard measures what the inference needs.}
A prediction is scored by its relative error on the held-out rows: the error of $M$, and for the charge the error of $\Dch$ rebuilt with the true mass, so that the charge target $Y = \log_{10}(\Dch/M)$ is judged on the quantity the inference uses. The errors are summarized by their median, 95th percentile and maximum, and read as significant figures (Section~\ref{sec:error-metric}). They are reported per zone -- the interior test curves, the edge curves held out of a refit, the rows at the physical edge -- and per decade of $\Dch$, because a mean over six orders of magnitude hides the weakest charges. Beside the error stands the ripple, the root mean square of the second difference of the residual across neighbouring grid values, near zero for a smooth surrogate and not for a lookup.

\paragraph{The search eliminates candidates in a fixed order.}
The five axes of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} are searched in that order, the representation first, then the families, their tuning and the precision regime. Each tuning search runs under a time budget of $\knobTuningBudgetMinutes$ minutes, and a change is kept only when its gain exceeds the spread of the fold and seed scores.

\paragraph{Every tuned candidate is scored in three zones and passes four gates.}
The gates are defined in Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}. The zones split the held-out rows: the interior test curves; the edge curves of the coupling grid, scored from a refit made without them (Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}); and the rows at the physical edge, nearest the existence edge of a black-hole curve and the stable edge of a neutron-star curve. \Cref{tab:zones} gives every tuned candidate of \cref{tab:tuning} in each zone beside the ceiling of its pair, and four gates read from it. The physics gate counts the reversed steps of the test curves, those whose predicted slope has another sign than the slope of the data, and counts a flat step, a predicted difference of zero where the data move, apart, since the prediction of boosted trees is piecewise constant along a curve. The interior gate is the score of Section~\ref{sec:error-metric}. The edge gate sets the margin of a map (Section~\ref{sec:validity}): it holds on the black holes, \zoneBhMassMargin{} on the mass and \zoneBhChargeMargin{} on the charge, and fails on the neutron stars, \zoneNsMassMargin{} on the mass and \zoneNsChargeMargin{} on the charge, where the extrapolation did not hold. The cost gate is the time budget of $\knobTuningBudgetMinutes$ minutes a search, recorded as the verdict of each candidate (Section~\ref{sec:precision}). A candidate too slow for a search is put on fixed points or cut, never dropped unreported; the verdicts are those of Section~\ref{sec:results}.
