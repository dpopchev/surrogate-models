\subsection{Tuning and Numerical Precision of the Survivors}
\label{sec:precision}

Each candidate on the short list of Section~\ref{sec:families} is tuned with the same budget: $40$ trials of the tree-structured Parzen estimator of Optuna~\citep{TODO_Optuna} over its own hyperparameters, the first ten drawn at random. The interpolant searches its number of neighbours from $50$ to $400$; the nearest-neighbour regressor $k$ from $2$ to $64$ and its weighting; the Gaussian process its kernel, squared-exponential, Mat\'ern~5/2 or rational quadratic; XGBoost its tree depth, number of trees and learning rate; the residual network its width from $32$ to $512$ units, its depth from $2$ to $8$ blocks, its activation, loss and a learning rate from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$. A curve-wise candidate also searches the knots of its spline, from $6$ to $24$ against the screen's eight. A trial is scored on the frozen folds alone, as the mean of their 95th-percentile figures, so the test curves never steer the search; a fit past the $30$-minute wall of the screen fails its trial. The best trial is refitted through the harness and scored once on the test curves. \Cref{tab:tuning} sets both beside the screen's figures and the data ceiling of each pair.

The tuning moves the curve-wise black-hole candidates most and the others little. On the black holes the curve-wise interpolant and Gaussian process each gain about two thirds of a figure, from $3.14$ to $3.79$ on the mass and from $2.28$ to $2.97$ and $2.94$ on the charge, and all four settle on the most knots allowed, $24$, or $23$ for the Gaussian process on the charge: the spline along the curve, whose error arcs between the knots in Section~\ref{sec:families}, still bounds them. The pointwise black-hole candidates gain at most $0.07$ figures, XGBoost none, and the pointwise interpolant takes the fewest neighbours allowed, $50$ and $51$. On the neutron stars the interpolant gains at most a tenth of a figure in either unit; pointwise it takes nearly the most neighbours allowed, $399$ on the mass and $377$ on the charge. In every curve-wise search the knots carry most of the variance of the trials' figures, an importance of $0.87$ to $1.00$ in the functional analysis of variance of Optuna, against at most $0.13$ for the base model's own setting.

The residual network gains most among the neutron-star candidates, from $0.72$ to $1.10$ figures on the charge with $90$ units over $5$ blocks, GELU and mean squared error, and on the test curves it keeps $1.16$, level with the curve-wise interpolant's $1.13$. Its five best trials are all narrow, $34$ to $90$ units over $5$ or $6$ blocks with mean squared error and a learning rate from $1.3 \times 10^{-4}$ to $3.3 \times 10^{-4}$; the learning rate and the width share most of the variance of its figures, $0.36$ each. It is also the only search the wall cuts short: $15$ of its $40$ trials ran past it, nine of the ten with $250$ units or more and six narrower ones with a learning rate below $2.5 \times 10^{-4}$, whose fits had not stopped early within the $30$ minutes. The tenth wide trial, $407$ units over $2$ blocks, finished with $-0.10$ figures. The walled trials took $12$ of the $33$ hours of its fits, and its search ran for about $16$ hours after the other thirteen candidates had finished.

After the tuning only the neutron-star mass comes within one figure of its ceiling: the curve-wise interpolant keeps $3.41$ figures on the folds and $3.58$ on the test curves against $\nsEdaCeilMFigures$. The neutron-star charge stays about one figure short of $\nsEdaCeilDFigures$, at $1.26$ on the folds; the black holes stay about three figures short, at $3.79$ against $\bhEdaCeilMFigures$ on the mass and $2.97$ against $\bhEdaCeilDFigures$ on the charge. On the folds of every pair the best tuned candidate is the curve-wise interpolant, tied on the black-hole mass by the curve-wise Gaussian process.

The cost of the tuning lies in the pointwise units and the network. A trial of the pointwise interpolant takes about a quarter of an hour, $7$ to $10$ hours a search, since each query solves its own system on up to $400$ neighbours; a curve-wise search takes at most $12$ minutes, and a trial of the residual network about $50$ minutes. Every fit runs on one thread, the three folds of a trial one after another, three searches at a time.

This tuning is a first pass, and its results name the next one. Where a best trial sits on the edge of its range, the search has not yet found the best of its family: the knots of the curve-wise black-hole candidates sit at the top of their range, and the neighbours of the pointwise interpolant at the bottom on the black holes and the top on the neutron stars. The next pass therefore widens the knots above $24$ and the neighbours below $50$ and above $400$; it narrows the residual network to about $128$ units, where all its best trials lie; it stops a trial whose first fold falls well short of the best so far; and it fits the three folds of a trial in parallel, which divides the cost of the searches that dominate it by about three. The precision regime below then runs on the candidates that pass.
