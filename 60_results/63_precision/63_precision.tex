\subsection{Tuning and Numerical Precision of the Survivors}
\label{sec:precision}

Each candidate on the short list of Section~\ref{sec:families} is tuned with the same budget: $40$ trials of the tree-structured Parzen estimator of Optuna~\citep{TODO_Optuna} over its own hyperparameters, the first ten drawn at random. The interpolant searches its number of neighbours from $50$ to $400$; the nearest-neighbour regressor $k$ from $2$ to $64$ and its weighting; the Gaussian process its kernel, squared-exponential, Mat\'ern~5/2 or rational quadratic; XGBoost its tree depth, number of trees and learning rate; the residual network its width from $32$ to $512$ units, its depth from $2$ to $8$ blocks, its activation, loss and a learning rate from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$. A curve-wise candidate also searches the knots of its spline, from $6$ to $24$ against the screen's eight. A trial is scored on the frozen folds alone, as the mean of their 95th-percentile figures, so the test curves never steer the search; a fit past the $30$-minute wall of the screen fails its trial. The best trial is refitted through the harness and scored once on the test curves. \Cref{tab:tuning} sets both beside the screen's figures and the data ceiling of each pair.

The tuning moves the curve-wise black-hole candidates most and the others little. On the black holes the curve-wise interpolant and Gaussian process each gain about two thirds of a figure, from $3.14$ to $3.79$ on the mass and from $2.28$ to $2.97$ and $2.94$ on the charge, and all four settle on the most knots allowed, $24$, or $23$ for the Gaussian process on the charge: the spline along the curve, whose error arcs between the knots in Section~\ref{sec:families}, still bounds them. The pointwise black-hole candidates gain at most $0.07$ figures, XGBoost none, and the pointwise interpolant takes the fewest neighbours allowed, $50$ and $51$. On the neutron stars the interpolant gains at most a tenth of a figure in either unit; pointwise it takes nearly the most neighbours allowed, $399$ on the mass and $377$ on the charge. The residual network gains most among the neutron-star candidates, from $0.72$ to $1.10$ figures on the charge with $90$ units over $5$ blocks, and on the test curves it keeps $1.16$, level with the curve-wise interpolant's $1.13$. Its search is the costliest of the batch: $15$ of its $40$ trials ran past the wall, nine of them with $250$ units or more and the other six with a learning rate below $2.5 \times 10^{-4}$, and it ran for about $16$ hours after the other thirteen candidates had finished.

After the tuning only the neutron-star mass comes within one figure of its ceiling: the curve-wise interpolant keeps $3.41$ figures on the folds and $3.58$ on the test curves against $\nsEdaCeilMFigures$. The neutron-star charge stays about one figure short of $\nsEdaCeilDFigures$, at $1.26$ on the folds; the black holes stay about three figures short, at $3.79$ against $\bhEdaCeilMFigures$ on the mass and $2.97$ against $\bhEdaCeilDFigures$ on the charge. On every pair the best tuned candidate is the curve-wise interpolant, tied on the black-hole mass by the curve-wise Gaussian process. Where a best trial sits on the edge of its range -- the knots of the curve-wise black-hole candidates, the neighbours of the pointwise interpolant -- the search has not yet found the best of its family, and that range is the first to widen.
