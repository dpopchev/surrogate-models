\subsection{Model Families and the Scheme of Elimination}
\label{sec:families}

Six model families are screened on the representation of Section~\ref{sec:representation}: the $k$-nearest-neighbour regressor and the local thin-plate interpolant of that section, Gaussian process regression~\citep{Rasmussen2005_GP} with a scaled squared-exponential kernel plus white noise, gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost~\citep{TODO_XGBoost}), the baseline multilayer perceptron, and a residual network of the same width and depth whose blocks add to identity skips. Each family runs in both units of prediction, one row at a time and curve-wise, where it learns the spline coefficients and the range of each curve from the curve parameters; the curve-wise interpolant is the interpolation between curves of the outline. The twelve candidates per dataset and target are eliminated by successive halving over the training rows: every candidate is scored through the harness of Section~\ref{sec:optimization} on $10^3$ training rows, the best three of each unit on the frozen folds go on to $10^4$, and the best two of each unit to $10^5$. Each unit is ranked apart: on $10^3$ rows the neutron-star design keeps about a hundred of its curves, too few for a curve-wise base, which at full data was the best representation of Section~\ref{sec:representation}. A smaller budget keeps rows evenly spaced along every curve, at least ten per curve, and fewer whole curves when the rows do not suffice for all. Each candidate has a fixed budget, its default settings and one seed; a fit past $30$ minutes or $4$\,GB is the family's wall at that number of rows, and a pointwise Gaussian process whose kernel matrix would pass the memory budget is not run. \Cref{tab:families} reports the significant figures each candidate keeps on the folds at the most rows it reached; Appendix~\ref{app:families} describes each family, its cost and what to look for in its row.

The local thin-plate interpolant survives on every pair and leads three of the four. On $10^5$ rows it keeps $3.31$ figures curve-wise and $3.28$ pointwise on the neutron-star mass, $1.25$ and $1.11$ on its charge, and $3.29$ pointwise on the black-hole mass, where the curve-wise Gaussian process and interpolant keep $3.14$; on the black-hole charge the curve-wise Gaussian process and interpolant tie at $2.28$. For every candidate of the last round the test curves agree with the folds within a quarter of a figure, so the ranking is not an artefact of the folds. Within one figure of each pair's best stay one family on the neutron-star mass, the interpolant in both units; three on its charge, with the residual network at $0.72$ and the curve-wise nearest neighbours at $0.32$; and three on each black-hole target, with XGBoost at $2.73$ and $1.76$ beside the interpolant and the curve-wise Gaussian process. The short list for the tuning of Section~\ref{sec:precision} therefore never holds more than three families per pair.

The pointwise Gaussian process meets its wall on $10^4$ rows on all four pairs: a single fit runs past $30$ minutes, as the cube of the rows foretells. Fitted curve-wise, on one row per curve, it takes seconds and ties for the lead on the black-hole charge. The curve-wise unit needs rows along every curve as well as curves: on the neutron stars it stays below zero figures up to $10^4$ rows, about ten per curve, and leads only on $10^5$, about seventy per curve. At their fixed budget the networks keep at most $1.65$ figures, the perceptron on the neutron-star mass after a fit of $217$ seconds, against less than two seconds for the interpolant; they reach the short list only on the neutron-star charge, where every family stays near one figure. Between the two units of the interpolant the figures are close but the ripple is not: on the neutron-star mass the curve-wise residual ripples about thirty times more across $\beta$, and five times more across $\lambda$, than the pointwise one, since each curve is rebuilt on its own. Against the data ceilings of $\nsEdaCeilMFigures$ and $\nsEdaCeilDFigures$ figures, the neutron-star survivors stay about one figure short on both targets; the black-hole survivors stay about four figures short of $\bhEdaCeilMFigures$ and $\bhEdaCeilDFigures$.
