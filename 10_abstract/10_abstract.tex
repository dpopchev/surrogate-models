\begin{abstract}
Solving the field equations of extended scalar-tensor-Gauss--Bonnet gravity inside Bayesian inference needs $10^5$--$10^7$ evaluations, a severe computational bottleneck~\citep{Reed2024}. We present a framework whose algorithm is a data processing pipeline that builds surrogates of compact-object maps which minimize the relative error on the interior of the dataset, extrapolate across the couplings only as far as held-out edge curves show the extrapolation holds, and keep the physics: past a physical edge the surrogate refuses the input with its reason and returns the nearest valid one (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}). The same pipeline is demonstrated on the two-parameter map $(\rh, \beta) \to (M, \Dch)$ of scalarized black holes~\citep{Doneva2017_BH} and on the three-parameter map $(\beta, \lambda, \rhoc) \to (M, \Dch)$ of scalarized neutron stars~\citep{Doneva2017_NS}. At the 95th percentile of the relative error on held-out curves, the black-hole mass reaches \resultsBhMassTest{} significant figures against a data ceiling of \resultsBhMassCeiling{}, and its charge \resultsBhChargeTest{} against \resultsBhChargeCeiling{}; the neutron-star mass reaches \resultsNsMassTest{} against \resultsNsMassCeiling{}, and its charge \resultsNsChargeTest{} against \resultsNsChargeCeiling{}. Every pair stays short of its ceiling. On the held-out edge curves the black-hole extrapolation holds, and that surrogate answers \validityBhMarginSteps{} step of the coupling grid beyond the sampled couplings with a warning; on the neutron stars it did not hold, so that surrogate has a margin of \validityNsMarginSteps{} steps and refuses beyond the sampled couplings (Section~\ref{sec:validity}).
\end{abstract}
