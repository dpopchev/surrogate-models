\subsection{Mechanism of Curvature-Induced Spontaneous Scalarization}
\label{sec:scalarization}

For the general-relativistic solutions with a trivial scalar field, $\phi = 0$, to remain solutions of the theory, the coupling function must satisfy~\citep{Doneva2017_BH}
\begin{equation}
  \left. \frac{df}{d\phi} \right|_{\phi = 0} = 0
  \quad \text{and} \quad
  \left. \frac{d^2 f}{d\phi^2} \right|_{\phi = 0} = \epsilon = \pm 1.
  \label{eq:coupling-conditions}
\end{equation}
Under these conditions the general-relativistic configurations with $\phi = 0$ are exact solutions of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:field-metric} and~\eqref{eq:field-scalar}~\citep{Doneva2017_BH,Doneva2017_NS}. Whether they are stable is another matter. A linear scalar perturbation $\delta\phi$ on a fixed general-relativistic background, marked $(*)$, obeys\review{cite: the linear stability analysis of the general-relativistic solutions}
\begin{equation}
  \Box^{(*)} \delta\phi + \frac{1}{4} \lambda^2 \epsilon\, \mathcal{R}^{2\,(*)}_{\mathrm{GB}} \delta\phi = 0.
  \label{eq:perturbation}
\end{equation}
Cast into the one-dimensional Schr\"odinger-like form $d^2 u / dr_*^2 + \left[ \omega^2 - U(r) \right] u = 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:perturbation} shows that a sufficiently large curvature term $\RGB$ digs a deep negative well into the effective potential $U(r)$\review{cite: the effective-potential analysis}. Once the potential is negative enough, $\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} U(r_*)\, dr_* < 0$, the trivial solution $\phi = 0$ develops a tachyonic instability, $\omega^2 < 0$~\citep{DamourEspositoFarese1993}. The system then scalarizes spontaneously: it bifurcates away from general relativity onto a new, energetically favourable branch with a non-trivial scalar profile $\phi(r) \neq 0$~\citep{Doneva2017_BH,Doneva2017_NS}.
