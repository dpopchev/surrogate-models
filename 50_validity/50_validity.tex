\section{Domain of Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

A surrogate of a compact-object map is meaningful only where stable solutions exist. For black holes that is a horizon radius $\rh$ at or above the existence edge $r_{h,\min}(\beta)$; for neutron stars it is the stable branch, up to the maximum mass of each $(\beta, \lambda)$; for both, the sampled coupling ranges (Section~\ref{sec:domain-boundaries}). Outside this domain the surrogate does not extrapolate: it refuses the query and returns the nearest valid input.

Figure~\ref{fig:validity-domain} sends a regular grid of probes, reaching beyond the sampled ranges, through the saved surrogates. The black-hole map answers \validityBhAnswered{} probes and refuses \validityBhRefused{}; the neutron-star map answers \validityNsAnswered{} and refuses \validityNsRefused{}. A neutron-star probe inside the sampled $(\beta, \lambda)$ region can still be refused: its $\rhoc$ may lie beyond the maximum mass of that curve, or its couplings between sampled curves, off their hull. Every held-out row lies inside the domain: \validityBhTestInside\% of the \validityBhTestRows{} black-hole and \validityNsTestInside\% of the \validityNsTestRows{} neutron-star test rows.
